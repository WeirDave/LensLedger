## Why this release

Trashing one photo showed an **Undo** button, but **Trash selected** on a
batch did not: it moved up to 500 photos to the Review Bin and showed only
a status line, so a mis-click on a large selection meant finding each photo
again in **Trash & restore**. The new **Exact duplicates** view makes bulk
trashing a routine step, so it needed the same safety net.

## What changed

**Trash selected**

- After **Trash selected** moves a batch to the Trash, a toast reads
  "Moved N photo(s) to Trash." with an **Undo** button. It stays for 30
  seconds, longer than the 12 seconds a single photo gets, because a batch
  is harder to re-select.
- **Undo** puts every photo of that batch back where it was and reloads the
  page.
- A photo that cannot be put back does not stop the others: each photo is
  tried and the rest are restored. A message names up to three that failed, with
  the reason (for example, a file now sits at its old location), and says
  they remain in **☰ → Trash & restore**.
- If every photo of a batch was selected on the page, the empty page now
  stays up with the toast instead of reloading, so **Undo** is still within
  reach.

**User manual**

- The Review Bin section describes the toast for one photo and for a batch.

## Verified

- 2 new tests: a bulk trash undone in one step (files back on disk, none
  left in the Trash, nothing left unrestored in the record); one photo that
  cannot be put back leaving the other restored and being named.
- The undo test was checked to fail with the restore removed.
- In Chromium against a synthetic 30-photo library: Ctrl+click two
  thumbnails, **Trash selected**, the toast reading "Moved 2 photo(s) to
  Trash.", **Undo**; the library showed 30 photos again, with no script
  errors.
