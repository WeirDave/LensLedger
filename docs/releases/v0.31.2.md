The Photo map's world map was a hand-drawn placeholder — flat rectangular
continents built from a handful of straight-line paths, labeled "Simplified
world map" in its own `aria-label`. This replaces it with real coastline
geometry adapted from "World location map (equirectangular 180).svg" by
TUBS on Wikimedia Commons (CC BY-SA 3.0 / GFDL): same recognizable shape
readers actually expect when they look at a world map. The source SVG's
rivers, country borders, and template legend marker were stripped out —
LensLedger only needed the coastline outlines to place a `#world` div's
background behind location markers — and colors/stroke styling were
changed to match the app's existing dark theme. The coastline path data
itself is unmodified from the source.

CC BY-SA requires attribution for adapted work, so that's now recorded in
two places: a `<!-- -->` comment at the top of `assets/world-map.svg`
itself, and a short credit line at the bottom of the map's legend
(`web/css/map.css`'s new `.map-credit` class) linking back to the original
Wikimedia file. `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md` also gained an entry describing
what was adapted and what was changed.

Alongside that, both places in the app that show a GPS coordinate — the
Photo map's marker details panel (`web/js/map.js`) and a photo's embedded
metadata panel in the viewer (`web/js/viewer.js`, fed by
`src/metadata_reader.py`'s new `osmHref` field) — now offer an "Open in
OpenStreetMap" link alongside the existing link to LensLedger's own local
map. That gives a way to cross-check a location (street-level detail,
searching nearby, etc.) without leaving the coordinate lookup to guesswork.
