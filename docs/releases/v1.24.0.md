# Looser look-alike matching where you review, strict where you don't

## Why this release

The cutoff report in 1.23.0 was run on a real library with tens of thousands of
unnamed faces, and it showed that the similarity LensLedger used to call two
faces "alike" was too strict for it.

- Two faces of the same person typically scored about 0.6 against each other, and
  the middle half of pairs scored between about 0.55 and 0.68. At the 0.72 cutoff
  only about one same-person pair in eight was reached (about one in two for the
  faces named earliest).
- Two different named people almost never scored alike: a typical pair scored
  about 0.02. About one face in a thousand (three in a thousand among the faces
  named earliest) had any face of a different named person at 0.5 or above, and
  none did at 0.6.
- So "Also looks like" groups stayed small, and most of the work stayed manual.

The same numbers say where a looser cutoff is safe and where it is not. Where you
look at the faces first, a wrong one costs an untick. Where nothing is looked at,
a wrong one is confirmed unseen.

## What changed

### Review similarity is now 0.60

- The faces offered for you to look at use a similarity of **0.60**, down from
  0.72, in three places:
  - the "Also looks like …" group after you name a face;
  - the faces offered for review after "Confirm all" (they were 0.72 up to 0.75,
    and are now 0.60 up to 0.75, highest first, up to 200);
  - the grouping behind the "+N similar" badges and their order on the People
    page. It uses the same value as the group, so a badge still equals the group
    you get when that card is named.
- Matches under 0.72 are outlined with a dashed border and counted in the group's
  header ("2 lower matches, outlined — check them"). Hovering one shows its score.
  They are ticked like the rest, as before, so check them before "Confirm all".

### What did not change

Everything that confirms a face without anyone looking keeps its cutoff:

| Step | Cutoff |
|---|---|
| "Confirm all remaining" | 0.72 |
| Faces confirmed automatically after "Confirm all" | 0.75 |
| Automatic confirmation when learning from named people | 0.90 |
| Suggestions from learning, which you review | 0.70 |

- The number on the "Confirm all remaining (N)" button counts only the faces it
  will confirm (0.72 and above). With a wider review band it would otherwise have
  counted every face on offer and overstated what the button does.

### Cutoff report

- Different-person pairs are counted over up to two million pairs, in chunks, so
  a rate that is very small can still be seen. When none was seen the report
  shows an upper bound such as `<0.00015%` instead of `0.0%`.
- A new "At your scale" table gives the wrong faces one face would meet among your
  unnamed faces, if they differed from it the way your named people differ from
  each other. It is only as good as that assumption: strangers and look-alike
  relatives who are not named are not measured.
- The cutoffs marked "current" now come from the values the app uses, so the
  report cannot drift from the app.
- The notes under the table now say that neither block of named faces is an
  unbiased sample. Faces confirmed automatically were confirmed because they
  scored high, and an early face is often named together with the faces that
  matched it, so real same-person scores are likely lower than shown.

## What to expect

- Bigger "Also looks like" groups, with more wrong faces in them. The wrong ones
  sit at the end, outlined. Untick them before "Confirm all".
- More faces will have a look-alike. On the library measured, about 22% of unnamed faces
  had one at 0.72 and about 40% at 0.60. Most unnamed faces still have none even at 0.5,
  so many of them are probably strangers or poor crops, which no cutoff fixes.
- The wrong-match figures cover only people you have named. Wrong faces among
  unnamed strangers are not measured, so treat them as a floor.

## Measurements

On generated test data (invented identities, no real photos), 36,000 faces in 400
uneven groups, first 30 cards, built so that two faces of one person score about
0.6 against each other, as in the real library.

| | 1.23.1 (0.72) | 1.24.0 (0.60) |
|---|---|---|
| Groups found among the first 30 cards | none (every badge 0) | 30, the biggest 3,629 |
| Distinct people among those cards | 24 | 28 |
| Photos belonging to the people shown | 12,950 | 18,738 |
| Time to build the page | 1.51 s | 0.88 s |

The test data spreads the scores more evenly than a real library does, so the real
gain will be smaller than this. The page-time difference is within what repeated
runs vary by and is not a promised speed-up.

## Verified

- Full test suite: 427 tests. 5 of them (publishing tests in `test_server.py`)
  error on the Linux machine this was developed on because the bundled
  `ExifTool.exe` is a Windows program. They error identically on 1.23.1. The other
  422 ran: 392 passed and 30 were skipped.
- New tests cover a face being offered down to 0.60 and no lower, matches under
  0.72 being flagged, a card's badge equalling the group offered once it is named,
  the review band after "Confirm all" (a face at 0.75 or above confirmed, faces
  from 0.60 to 0.75 offered and not confirmed, a face under 0.60 left out), the
  button count including only faces at 0.72 or above, "Confirm all remaining"
  still stopping at 0.72, and the report's upper bound, exact counts for people
  who score alike, scale table, and cutoff markers.
- Making "Confirm all remaining" use the review value made its test fail, and
  counting the whole review band in the button made the review-band test fail.
  Both passed again once restored.
- The People page was driven in Chromium in the light and dark themes against the
  real server: a group of five matches showed two outlined lower matches, the header
  counted them, and every match was still ticked.
